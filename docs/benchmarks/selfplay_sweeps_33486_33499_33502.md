# Dummy-MCTS self-play sweeps

All jobs used immutable schema-v3 chunks, no root noise, `c_puct=1.5`, and
independent seeds.  Every chunk below was checksum-loaded and every game was
replayed through its recorded terminal winner after generation.

## Mini board: job 33486

Each configuration contains 64 games.  Six CPU-only array tasks ran with a
four-task concurrency cap and used 2.4 MB in total.

| Rollout | Simulations | Positions | Seconds | Positions/s | P1 win fraction | Normalized policy entropy | Top-visit share | Mean abs(soft-Z - result) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Uniform | 16 | 941 | 6.38 | 147.45 | 0.391 | 0.694 | 0.421 | 0.895 |
| Uniform | 32 | 887 | 8.01 | 110.78 | 0.516 | 0.800 | 0.361 | 0.856 |
| Uniform | 64 | 798 | 10.63 | 75.08 | 0.625 | 0.685 | 0.466 | 0.813 |
| Tactical | 16 | 896 | 6.31 | 142.09 | 0.469 | 0.690 | 0.436 | 0.756 |
| Tactical | 32 | 879 | 8.13 | 108.16 | 0.578 | 0.788 | 0.371 | 0.730 |
| Tactical | 64 | 884 | 11.31 | 78.20 | 0.563 | 0.681 | 0.462 | 0.687 |

Tactical rollouts had negligible throughput cost in this run and lower
soft-Z/result disagreement at every search budget.  Combined with their
earlier equal-time Elo advantage, tactical became the standard-data default.
Thirty-two simulations produced the most diffuse mini targets.

## First standard-board probe: job 33499

Eight games per setting established scale only:

| Simulations | Positions | Seconds | Positions/s | Mean game plies | P1 wins | Policy entropy | Soft-Z error |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 16 | 432 | 9.44 | 45.77 | 54.0 | 0/8 | 0.391 | 1.076 |
| 32 | 397 | 13.17 | 30.15 | 49.6 | 1/8 | 0.554 | 0.933 |
| 64 | 446 | 23.80 | 18.74 | 55.8 | 2/8 | 0.743 | 0.900 |

The outcome skew triggered two independent diagnostics.  Five thousand raw
uniform and five thousand raw tactical rollouts were approximately balanced,
and a deterministic player-swap test proved whole-search visit equivalence and
Q antisymmetry.  The probe was therefore expanded instead of being diagnosed
as a sign bug.

## Standard-board stage one: job 33502

| Simulations | Games | Positions | Seconds | Positions/s | Mean plies | P1 win fraction | Policy entropy | Top share | Soft-Z error |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 32 | 64 | 4,111 | 138.79 | 29.62 | 64.2 | 0.234 | 0.566 | 0.416 | 0.943 |
| 64 | 64 | 3,689 | 205.16 | 17.98 | 57.6 | 0.453 | 0.735 | 0.302 | 0.890 |

The 64-simulation data costs about 65 percent more CPU per position, but has
much more distributed policy targets, a more balanced outcome sample, and
slightly better root-value agreement.  Equal-position neural training and Elo,
not these diagnostics alone, decide whether that cost is worthwhile.

Full replay validation of the 128 standard games took about 136 seconds—close
to one generator shard's runtime.  Validation cost must therefore be budgeted
and parallelized when raw archives grow; it is not free bookkeeping.
